2026 Midterm Election Results

Governors, Senate, and House results from Election Day, Tuesday, November 3, 2026, drawn as cartograms: states sized by 2020 Census population, House districts as equal tiles. Colour is the winning party — blue for Democrats, red for Republicans — shaded by the winner’s vote share in five bins, pale under 55% and deepest at 85% or more.

Results as of Sunday, November 8, 2026, ~4:30 p.m. PT — five days after Election Day. Results are unofficial; states certify in the coming weeks, and a “call” is a news organisation’s projection, not an official result. 23 races are still uncalled, and two Louisiana House seats go to runoffs on December 12.

Sources: Wikipedia’s 2026 gubernatorial, Senate, and House election pages (revisions of Nov 8), cross-checked against AP call status, Ballotpedia, and Poliwave. House vote shares are AP data last updated Nov 5–6.

View 01 · Governors

Governors — 36 races

Each square is a state, sized by its 2020 Census population (a Demers cartogram). Democrats flipped five governorships — Georgia, Iowa, Nevada, Ohio, and Texas — and hold a majority of governorships for the first time since 2009: 28–20 overall, with Alaska and Kansas still to come.

Democrats 22 · Republicans 12 · 2 uncalled (AK, KS) · 14 states had no race

The 14 grey states — DE, IN, KY, LA, MS, MO, MT, NJ, NC, ND, UT, VA, WA, WV — elect their governors in other years. Alaska is ranked-choice: the shares shown are first-choice votes, not a final margin. Kansas, at 0.6 points with ~99% counted, is the closest governor’s race in the country.

View 02 · Senate

Senate — 35 seats up

Same cartogram, coloured only where a seat was contested: 33 Class 2 seats plus Class 3 special elections in Florida and Ohio. Democrats flipped seven seats — Iowa, Kansas, Maine, North Carolina, Ohio (special), South Carolina, and Texas — and lost none of their own. The chamber stands 54–44 on called races, with Alaska and Mississippi pending.

Democrats 20 · Republicans 13 · 2 uncalled (AK, MS) · 15 states had no seat up

Florida’s special election was the closest called Senate race: Ashley Moody (R) held it 50.9–48.0. The Florida and Ohio winners serve to January 2029; the other 33 seats run to January 2033. Alaska’s Senate race is ranked-choice and no vote shares have been published yet; Mississippi has published none either.

View 03 · House

House — 435 districts

Every voting district is one equal tile, laid out to keep neighbours neighbours (the squared-cost tile grid). Democrats won control of the House; only two seats flipped Republican all night — TN-09 and TX-32. If all 19 current leaders held, the House would finish 261–174.

Democrats 254 · Republicans 162 · 19 uncalled · popular vote D 55.7% / R 42.8%

435 voting districts only — the six non-voting delegates (DC, Puerto Rico, and the territories) are excluded. California accounts for four of the uncalled races, mostly mail-ballot backlog rather than close counts; MN-01, NC-03, and NC-05 are effectively tied.

Reading the fine print

What is still shaky

  • House vote shares lag the calls. The percentages come from AP data last updated Nov 5–6, while the calls are as of Nov 8 — so a share may have moved by about a point since. The winners will not have.
  • Three called races the AP had not called (as of its Nov 6 data): FL-09 (Soto, D, +0.6), TN-04 (Broderick, D, +0.2), and ME-02 (LePage, R, +1.6). The first two are genuine recount risks.
  • FL-10 has no published vote totals — the AP called it for Maxwell Frost (D) at poll close with 0% counted, so it is shaded at mid-depth with no margin.
  • Alaska is ranked-choice everywhere — governor, Senate, and its single House seat. First-choice shares are not final margins, and the Senate race has no published shares at all yet.
  • Louisiana decides two seats on December 12: LA-05 (Moore, D, vs Firment, R) and LA-06 (Banks, D, vs Miguez, R) go to runoffs after a jungle primary. Their tiles are marked as runoffs, not party wins.
  • Callers disagree by an hour and a seat or two. These tallies follow Wikipedia’s Nov 8 revisions; at the same moment the AP’s live tally differed by two or three seats in each chamber. That is caller lag, not an error in either.
