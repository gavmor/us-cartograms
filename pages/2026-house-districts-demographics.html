Who Lives in Each House District

The United States House of Representatives has 435 voting districts, each holding roughly the same number of people — about 760,000 after the 2020 Census — and each electing one member for a two-year term. This page shows who lives in each district, from the Census Bureau’s American Community Survey (ACS) 2024 1-year estimates, and how each district voted in the House elections of 3 November 2026.

The map below is a tile cartogram: every district is one equal square, laid out to keep neighbouring districts neighbours as far as the grid allows. Pick a measure from the menu and the squares change colour along a green-to-orange scale — pale green at the low end, burnt orange at the high end. The chart underneath plots the same measure against each district’s Democratic vote share in 2026. Hover or tap a square or a dot to see the district’s numbers and its 2026 result.

Demographics as of the ACS 2024 1-year estimates (Census Bureau, published September 2025), for the 119th Congress districts in use from 2023 to 2033. Election results as of 25 November 2026 — 432 of 435 races called; two Louisiana seats go to runoffs on 12 December and MN-01 awaits a hand recount. A “call” is a news organisation’s projection that a candidate has won, made before the count is finished, not an official result.

Sources: ACS 2024 1-year tables via Census Reporter (no key required); 2026 House results from the 2026 midterm results page; 2024 House winners by district from Wikipedia’s 2024 House elections page (for the seats that changed party). Read it with care. A pattern across districts is not proof about individual voters: a district can be wealthy and Democratic because of where its wealthy voters live, or because of who else lives there. District-level correlation is not individual behaviour.

Methods note

Where the numbers come from

  • Demographics: American Community Survey 2024 1-year estimates, Census Bureau, via Census Reporter. The ACS surveys a sample of households every year; the 1-year estimates are the freshest, but for a single congressional district they carry margins of error of a few percent on most shares (larger on small groups). The page shows each estimate’s margin of error on hover. Shares are computed from the published table cells; percentage margins of error use the Census Bureau’s proportion formula, with a root-sum-of-squares fallback in the few cases where that formula’s radicand went negative.
  • Boundaries: 119th Congress (in use 2023–2033), from the 2020 Census cartographic boundary files. District populations are the 2020 Census CD119 counts.
  • Election results: 2026 House elections, as recorded on the 2026 midterm results page on 25 November 2026. The Democratic vote share is the winner’s share where a Democrat won, otherwise the runner-up’s share. Called districts only: 432 of 435, minus FL-10, which was called with no published vote totals, leaving 431 dots. The two Louisiana runoffs and MN-01 are not plotted.
  • Seats that changed party compare the 2026 winner with the 2024 winner by district, taken from Wikipedia’s 2024 House elections page (per-state results tables). 51 seats changed hands: 48 from Republican to Democratic, 3 from Democratic to Republican (ME-02, TN-09, TX-32). The 2024 totals reconcile exactly (R 220 / D 215), and the flips reconcile with the 2026 called tally (215 − 1 uncalled D seat − 3 + 48 = 259).
  • Colour: the tile map uses the site’s green-to-orange sequential ramp (pale green to burnt orange), in eight quantile bins — each bin holds roughly an eighth of the 435 districts. Party colours (blue Democrat, red Republican) are used only for election outcomes, never for demographics.
